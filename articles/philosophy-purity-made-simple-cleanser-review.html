What to check before you buy

Your skin type: cleansers can feel different on dry, oily, combination, or reactive skin. If your skin tends to feel tight after washing, pay attention to how it feels after use and consider a gentler option if needed.

The exact formula: product names and formulas can vary by size or listing. Read the current ingredient panel, especially if you have known sensitivities or avoid particular ingredients.

Makeup expectations: the product is marketed to remove stubborn makeup, but no cleanser works identically for everyone or every product. If you use long-wear or waterproof makeup, check whether the directions recommend extra cleansing or a dedicated remover.

Price per ounce: compare the size and current price with other cleansers you’re considering. The best value depends on how much you use and how well it fits your routine.

A useful rule of thumb: a cleanser doesn’t need to promise a dramatic transformation. If it removes what you need it to remove, feels comfortable on your skin, and is easy to use consistently, it may earn its place on your shelf.

What does 7% glycolic acid actually mean?

Glycolic acid is an alpha hydroxy acid, or AHA. In skincare, AHAs are used to exfoliate the outer surface of the skin. That’s why glycolic acid products are often considered by people concerned about dull-looking skin or rough, uneven texture.

The 7% figure tells you the stated glycolic acid concentration, which can help when comparing exfoliating toners. But a percentage alone doesn’t determine whether a product is right for you. Your skin’s sensitivity, the rest of your routine, and the product’s full formula and directions all matter.

Three things to consider before using a glycolic acid toner

  • Use it as directed: follow the current product label for frequency and application. Don’t interpret the phrase “daily toner” as an instruction to use an exfoliating acid every day unless the official directions say so.
  • Avoid overdoing active ingredients: combining glycolic acid with retinoids, other exfoliating acids, or potentially irritating products may increase the chance of dryness or irritation.
  • Be consistent with sun protection: AHAs can increase skin’s sensitivity to the sun. Use sunscreen and follow the product’s sun-protection precautions.

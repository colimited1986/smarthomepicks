Air Fryer Buying Guide: Size, Style & Features to Check

A practical way to narrow the choices before you spend money on a countertop air fryer.

Our approach: We explain what to compare and what to verify on the product listing. We do not claim hands-on testing unless a guide explicitly says so.

Start with servings, not presets

Think about the amount of food you usually cook at once. A compact basket can work well for one or two people, while family portions may mean cooking in batches unless you choose a larger oven-style unit. Capacity labels are only a starting point: compare the usable basket or tray dimensions with the food and pans you actually use.

Basket or oven style?

Basket models usually take up less counter space and suit quick batches of fries, vegetables or reheated leftovers. Oven-style models offer racks and a wider cooking area, which can be useful for toast, trays or more than one food at a time. They also need more room and can take longer to clean.

Measure the space around it

Measure width, depth and height, then check the door or basket clearance. Leave the ventilation space specified by the manufacturer and make sure the appliance can sit on a heat-safe surface near a suitable outlet.

Check before checkout

  • Exact external dimensions and required ventilation clearance
  • Usable basket or tray dimensions, not capacity alone
  • Included racks, trays and accessories
  • Cleaning instructions for removable parts
  • Warranty, return terms and current customer feedback

Bottom line: A good air fryer is the one that fits your routine and kitchen—not necessarily the one with the most presets.
